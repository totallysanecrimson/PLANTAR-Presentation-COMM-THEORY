Audience-Centric Framework

Uses and Gratifications Theory (UGT)

Pioneered by Elihu Katz, Jay Blumler, and Michael Gurevitch, UGT shifts the foundational question from "What does media do to people?" to "What do people do with media?" It assumes audiences are active, goal-oriented consumers selecting media to satisfy specific psychological and social needs.

The 5 Classic Media Gratification Needs

Click cards for examples

1. Cognitive Needs

Acquiring information, knowledge, news, and intellectual curiosity.

2. Affective Needs

Emotional connection, pleasure, aesthetic enjoyment, and feeling.

3. Personal Integrative

Status, credibility, confidence, self-esteem, and identity validation.

4. Social Integrative

Belonging, connecting with family, peer talk, and social cohesion.

5. Tension Release

Escapism, stress relief, relaxation, and passing time.

Practical Application

Case Studies & Synthesis

Examine how the Communication Process Model and Uses & Gratifications Theory intersect in contemporary digital environments.

Case 1: Remote Work Miscommunication

Slack Text vs. Video Call Dynamics

A software engineering team suffers frequent project friction over Slack. Applying the **Communication Process Model**, text-only channels lack non-verbal encoding (tone, facial cues), introducing high **semantic noise**. By switching to video meetings, channel richness increases, providing immediate feedback loops.

Key Concept: Channel Capacity & Noise Reduction.
Case 2: Viral Social Media Trends

Why Do Memes Spread Fast?

Through **Uses & Gratifications Theory**, memes simultaneously fulfill **Personal Integrative** (showing cultural awareness), **Social Integrative** (bonding over shared humor), and **Tension Release** needs. Audiences actively re-encode and re-transmit memes as active participants rather than passive receivers.

Key Concept: Active Audience & Multi-Need Fulfillment.

Quick Knowledge Flashcards

Question 1 (Click to flip)
How does "Semantic Noise" differ from "Physical Noise"?
Physical noise is environmental sound/static. Semantic noise occurs when sender and receiver use different linguistic codes or jargon.
Question 2 (Click to flip)
What core paradigm shift did UGT introduce to mass comms?
It shifted focus from "What media does to people" (hypodermic needle effect) to "What people actively do with media".
Question 3 (Click to flip)
Why is the Feedback Loop critical in communication models?
Feedback transforms linear transmission into a dynamic two-way dialogue, allowing the sender to verify whether the message was accurately decoded.
